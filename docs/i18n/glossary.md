# Translation glossary and style

For every translation of GigChain Keys: the website now, the app and its
user guide next. Written for keyboard players, as one of them would say it
in their language: never word for word.

## Never translated

- **Names:** GigChain Keys, GigChain, MainStage, Gig Performer, Cantabile,
  Carla, Camelot, Ableton Live, Logic, REAPER, Arturia, Analog Lab,
  Keyscape, Kontakt, Surge XT, Vital, Dexed, FabFilter, Buy Me a Coffee,
  GitHub, GitHub Sponsors, Discord, YouTube, Windows, macOS, Linux, Ubuntu,
  Debian, Mint, PipeWire, JACK, ASIO, WASAPI, Apple Silicon.
- **Our instruments and effects:** Piano Pro, Pads Pro, Saw Stack Pro,
  Sustain Pads, Squash, Enhance, OTT.
- **Terms players use as they are, everywhere:** VST, VST2, VST3, MIDI,
  plugin, preset, setlist (where the language has no common word for it),
  Rhodes, Wurlitzer, pad, synth, supersaw, SoundFont, SF2, AppImage, .deb.
- **Controls named on screen**, when the app is not in that language yet:
  write the English name in bold, then explain it (**Perform**, the stage
  view). When the app is translated, use its translated names.

## Each language

| Language | Address the reader | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hindi (`hi`) | आप | Devanagari, everyday Hindi as musicians speak it. Music and computer terms in English (Latin letters) as players say them: keyboard, VST, MIDI, setlist, plugin, laptop, download. Avoid Sanskritised words nobody uses for this (not संगणक for computer). |
| Portuguese, Brazil (`pt-br`) | você | Brazilian usage (baixar, tecladista, teclado controlador, show, ao vivo). |
| Spanish (`es`) | tú | Neutral Spanish for Latin America and Spain alike (descargar, tecladista, en vivo, teclado controlador). |
| Indonesian (`id`) | Anda | Plain Indonesian; music terms in English as players use them (keyboardist, plugin, preset, setlist). |
| German (`de`) | du | Musicians' informal German (Keyboarder, live spielen, Bühne). |
| French (`fr`) | vous | Musicians' French (claviériste, en live, sur scène; plugin, preset as used). |
| Russian (`ru`) | вы | Musicians' Russian (клавишник, на сцене, вживую; VST, MIDI in Latin letters). |
| Japanese (`ja`) | です・ます | Natural Japanese; music terms as Japanese players write them (キーボーディスト, ライブ, プラグイン, プリセット, VST/MIDI in Latin letters). |
| Korean (`ko`) | 합니다/해요 polite | Natural Korean (키보디스트, 라이브, 플러그인, 프리셋; VST/MIDI in Latin letters). |
| Chinese, Simplified (`zh-cn`) | 你 | Mainland usage (键盘手, 现场演出, 插件, 预设; VST/MIDI in Latin letters). |

## Rules

- **Meaning, not words.** Each sentence says what the English means, in the
  way that language would say it. Short sentences stay short.
- **Keep every tag and link** in a phrase (`<b>`, `<a href>`): move them to
  where the translated words fall, never drop or change them.
- **Keep placeholders** (`{os}`, `{version}`) exactly.
- **Prices, sizes and versions** stay as numbers; dates and number formats
  follow the language.
- **Not a religious app:** no worship or hymn words, in any language.
- **Read it back.** After translating, read each phrase back into English
  and compare its meaning with the original; rewrite any phrase that
  drifted.
