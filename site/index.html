Everything a keys player needs on stage

Load your sounds once, and the whole night is ready.

Plays your plugins

Your VST3 and VST2 instruments and effects. Every sound in the setlist is loaded up front, so changing songs is instant and held notes ring on.

Setlists

All the night's songs in one file, in order, each with its own sounds. Change songs from the keyboard, a pedal or a pad.

Chord charts

Paste a song from any chord website and each chord lands on its word. Big enough to read from the keys.

Splits and layers

Key zones, transpose and velocity layers. Each part of the song (verse, chorus, solo) can switch which sounds play.

Perform mode

Full screen for the stage and touch screens: the chart, big Play, Next part and Loop part buttons that land on the bar line.

Its own instruments

Piano Pro (recorded grand, upright, Rhodes, Wurlitzer and more), Saw Stack Pro (EDM supersaws), Pads Pro (ambient pads under the band) and a SoundFont player for any .sf2. Plus Squash, Enhance and Sidechain effects. No plugin to buy.

Set up like a pro rig

Each keyboard's touch, tuning (A4 415–466 Hz), transpose the whole rig in one tap, your own plugin folders, autosave with backups, and a warning if the audio ever drops out.

Practice and warm-ups

The song's chords fall onto a keyboard, YouTube-piano style. Daily warm-ups at your level, every run scored.

Loop station

Record a loop of any instrument in time with the song and layer on top, street-performer style.

Built not to fail

Plugins are scanned in a separate process, a crashing plugin is switched off, and an unplugged keyboard comes back by itself.

How to run the AppImage

Make it executable, then run it: chmod +x GigChainKeys-*.AppImage. If it says FUSE is missing, install libfuse2.

You'll also want a MIDI keyboard. Pianos, synths and pads come built in, and free ones cover pianos, synths and pads. Every release lists checksums and notes on the releases page.

Questions

Is there MainStage for Windows?

No: Apple's MainStage only runs on a Mac. GigChain Keys does the same job on Windows (and on Mac and Linux too): it hosts your VST plugins, keeps a setlist of sounds for every song, layers and splits the keyboard, and switches sounds instantly on stage. It's free.

Is it really free?

Yes. No ads, no account, no licence key, no subscription. It's open source under the GPL, paid for by donations.

Which plugins work?

VST3 and VST2 instruments and effects: Kontakt, Arturia, Spitfire, u-he, Surge XT, Vital and the free ones. Plugins on another drive? Add the folder in Settings and click Rescan.

Does it work with my MIDI keyboard?

Any USB or MIDI keyboard. Its knobs, faders, pads and pedals can change songs, sounds and parts, and you can set how hard you have to play for full volume.

How is it different from Gig Performer or Cantabile?

They're excellent paid hosts. GigChain Keys is free and open source, adds chord charts, practice and its own instruments, and runs on Linux too. It's newer (a public alpha), so try it at home before a gig.

Does it run on Linux?

Yes: an AppImage for most distributions and a .deb for Ubuntu, Debian and Mint, with JACK, PipeWire and ALSA.
